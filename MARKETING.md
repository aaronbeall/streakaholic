# Streakaholic Marketing Plan

Streakaholic should be marketed first as a motivating streak tracker with light,
satisfying gamification. Privacy, flexible schedules, and the lack of ads or a
subscription are important supporting benefits, but they are not the lead story.

The promise is:

> Build streaks worth celebrating.

Streakaholic is for people who know what they want to do but have trouble staying
consistent. Completing a habit should feel rewarding: the streak advances, the app
responds, achievements accumulate, and progress becomes visible without turning life
into a complicated game.

## Positioning and messaging

### Message hierarchy

1. **Stay motivated by keeping streaks alive.** Make consistency visible and rewarding.
2. **Enjoy light gamification.** Celebrations, milestones, achievements, and a Trophy
   Case add encouragement without points, currencies, or competitive pressure.
3. **Track habits on schedules that fit real life.** Daily, selected weekdays, a number
   of days per week or month, and multiple completions per day.
4. **See progress clearly.** Calendars, charts, history, and aggregate statistics make
   improvement tangible.
5. **Keep it private and simple.** No account, cloud, tracking, ads, or subscription;
   data stays on the device and can be exported.

If [Commitment Mode](COMMITMENT_MODE.md) ships, it becomes a strong optional proof point
under the primary motivation message: users who want higher stakes can create a habit
whose past cannot be rewritten, earn visibly stronger achievement variants, and use a
fully disclosed Streak Save without falsifying the missed completion. Do not market this
as available until it is implemented and validated.

### Brand message system

- **Tagline:** Build streaks worth celebrating.
- **Category:** A playful, private habit & streak tracker.
- **Store short description:** Build habit streaks worth celebrating. Private by design,
  no account needed.

Use the tagline consistently rather than rotating among similar campaign lines. It is
the emotional hook; privacy should follow as trust-building proof. See [BRAND.md](BRAND.md)
for the canonical one-liner, voice, and product terminology.

### Short pitch

> Streakaholic is a playful, privacy-first habit tracker that keeps you motivated with
> satisfying streaks, celebrations, and achievements—while your data stays on your
> device and under your control.

### Language to emphasize

- Momentum
- Consistency
- Keep the streak alive
- Small wins
- Satisfying progress
- Milestones and achievements
- Playful, not demanding
- Encouragement without guilt

Avoid describing Streakaholic primarily as a productivity tool. It can improve
productivity, but its emotional benefit is motivation. Also avoid implying that users
are punished for missing a day; the tone should be encouraging rather than obsessive or
guilt-driven.

## Target audiences

Start with a narrow audience and expand after learning which message attracts retained
users.

### Primary audience

Android users who want to build personal routines and already respond well to streaks,
milestones, checklists, or visible progress. Examples include exercise, reading,
learning, hydration, medication, mindfulness, chores, and creative practice.

### Secondary audiences

- People who enjoy Duolingo-style streak motivation but do not want a full game
- People disappointed by habit trackers that feel clinical or joyless
- Users seeking a flexible alternative to strict daily-only streaks
- Privacy-conscious users who prefer local data and no account
- People tired of subscriptions for small personal utilities

## Success milestones

The initial goal is not maximum downloads. It is evidence that people install the app,
create real habits, and keep using it.

- 20 dependable closed-test participants
- 15 testers who use the app throughout a two-week test
- 10 useful feedback responses
- No known data-loss, reminder, or completion blockers
- 100 organic production installs
- 20–30 users still active after four weeks
- 10 honest Play Store reviews
- Several reusable user quotes describing motivation, satisfaction, or consistency

## Phase 2: Recruit testers

### Where to find them

- Friends, relatives, coworkers, classmates, and local acquaintances with Android phones
- Local Android, software, productivity, fitness, or self-improvement meetups
- Personal accounts on Bluesky, Mastodon, Threads, X, LinkedIn, or Facebook
- Relevant Discord communities where tester requests are allowed
- Indie Hackers and other indie-developer communities
- r/AndroidClosedTesting
- r/AlphaAndBetaUsers
- r/TestMyApp
- Tester-exchange communities such as LaunchCrew

Always read current community rules before posting. In particular, use the designated
self-promotion area for r/androidapps or share in r/droidappshowcase rather than posting
an unsolicited promotion in r/androidapps.

### Tester offer

Ask people to use two or three real habits for at least two weeks. Offer something that
recognizes their time:

- Free future Pro access
- Optional credit as a founding tester
- Early influence over features and polish
- Direct acknowledgement of the bugs or ideas they contributed

Suggested invitation:

> I am building Streakaholic, an Android habit tracker that uses streaks, celebrations,
> and achievements to make consistency more motivating. I am looking for people willing
> to track two or three real habits for two weeks and tell me what is confusing,
> satisfying, or annoying. It has no account, ads, tracking, or subscription, and early
> testers will receive free future Pro access. Interested?

### Testing cadence

- [ ] Day 1: confirm installation and ask the tester to create three genuine habits.
- [ ] Day 2: ask whether completion gestures and streak status made sense.
- [ ] Day 7: ask them to inspect the calendar, statistics, and Trophy Case.
- [ ] Day 10: ask them to try reminders and edit or archive a habit.
- [ ] Day 14: send the short survey and request a 15-minute conversation if useful.
- [ ] Record bugs, confusion, requests, positive reactions, and quotable language
      separately.
- [ ] Ask permission before using anyone's quote or name in marketing.
- [ ] Ask for a Play Store review only after the production release.

Useful survey questions:

1. What did you expect the app to do before opening it?
2. Did completing habits and extending streaks feel motivating?
3. Which celebration, achievement, or progress view felt most satisfying?
4. What was confusing or frustrating?
5. What would make you stop using it?
6. Would you recommend it, and how would you describe it to someone else?

## Phase 3: Create screenshots and demo videos

The purpose of a demo is not to tour every screen. Each video should communicate one
idea quickly and make the app feel satisfying.

### Capture preparation

- [ ] Use the realistic demo data so streaks, charts, and achievements look lived-in.
- [ ] Choose habits that viewers understand instantly: Read, Walk, Meditate, Drink Water,
      Practice Guitar, or Learn Spanish.
- [ ] Use a clean device or emulator with notifications and personal information hidden.
- [ ] Set the clock, battery, and network indicators to a visually clean state when
      possible.
- [ ] Choose either light or dark mode for a complete sequence; do not switch modes in
      the middle of a video.
- [ ] Turn on “Show taps” only for a technical tutorial. For marketing videos, use
      editing highlights or leave touches invisible.
- [ ] Record several takes of each action with one or two seconds of stillness before
      and after it. Those handles make editing much easier.
- [ ] Capture important animations at normal speed; slow them down during editing only
      when necessary.
- [ ] Record at the phone's native resolution and highest practical quality.

### Five useful raw clips

Build a reusable library instead of recording a new session for every post:

1. Press and hold to complete a habit; show the ring and streak response.
2. Trigger a new-best-streak or milestone celebration.
3. Open the Trophy Case and browse unlocked/in-progress achievements.
4. Flip or open a habit to reveal its calendar and statistics.
5. Create a habit with a flexible schedule, icon, color, and reminder.

Also capture a clean home-screen scroll and dashboard scroll. Store the unedited clips
with descriptive names such as `complete-reading-streak-dark.mp4` rather than leaving
them as anonymous screen-recording files.

### Recommended video formats

Create a small number of master edits that can be reused:

| Use | Aspect ratio | Suggested length | Goal |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| TikTok, Reels, Shorts | 9:16 | 10–25 seconds | One hook and one satisfying payoff |
| Social feed post | 4:5 or 1:1 | 10–30 seconds | Demonstrate one benefit without sound |
| Landing page | 9:16 or framed phone | 20–35 seconds | Explain the core motivation loop |
| Product Hunt/launch post | 16:9 or 1:1 | 30–60 seconds | Show the broader product story |
| Google Play preview | Follow current Play requirements | 30–60 seconds | Convert store visitors into installs |

Most people will see social videos muted. Captions and on-screen text must tell the
story without narration.

### Simple editing workflow

Phone-first editors such as CapCut, VN, or Canva are sufficient for short social videos.
DaVinci Resolve is a strong free desktop option when more precise editing or reusable
templates become worthwhile. Pick one tool and learn a repeatable workflow instead of
switching tools for every video.

1. **Start a project in the target aspect ratio.** Use 1080×1920 for a typical vertical
   social master.
2. **Import only the best takes.** Keep the full raw library outside the editing project.
3. **Trim aggressively.** Remove loading, hesitation, navigation, and repeated taps.
4. **Open with the payoff or problem.** The first one or two seconds need movement and a
   clear promise.
5. **Add large on-screen text.** Keep each card to one short sentence and away from the
   top/bottom areas covered by social-app controls.
6. **Use subtle zooms or a phone frame.** Enlarge the relevant UI without obscuring it.
7. **Add captions if there is narration.** Correct the automatic captions manually.
8. **Use restrained music and sound effects.** The app's visual feedback is the star;
   avoid turning a calm habit app into a noisy game advertisement.
9. **End with the app name and one action.** For example: “Keep your streak alive —
   Streakaholic for Android.”
10. **Export an H.264 MP4 at 1080p and 30 fps.** Use 60 fps only when the source was
    recorded at 60 fps and the animations materially benefit.
11. **Watch the export on a phone with sound off.** Check text size, pacing, cropping,
    spelling, and whether the promise is understandable.
12. **Save the editable project.** Swap the hook and ending later to create variants
    without rebuilding the video.

### Phone-screen presentation options

- **Full-screen crop:** The screen recording fills a vertical canvas. Fast and direct,
  but some UI may be cropped.
- **Centered phone frame:** Place the whole recording inside a device frame over a brand
  color or subtle gradient. This is safest for showing the complete interface.
- **Detail crop:** Zoom into the streak ring, badge, or achievement for a short payoff.
  Use this briefly, then show enough context that viewers understand what happened.

For Streakaholic, a centered phone recording on the brand navy (`#01073B`, see BRAND.md) with a
headline above it is a good default. Use detail crops for completions and celebrations.

### Batch-production routine

One focused hour can produce several posts:

- Record five to ten clean app interactions once.
- Make one 20-second master video.
- Duplicate it and test three opening hooks.
- Export vertical, square/4:5, and landing-page versions.
- Pull three still frames for image posts.
- Write one longer caption and adapt it for each community.
- Schedule the pieces across two weeks rather than posting them all at once.

Keep a small hook library:

- “The tiny animation that makes me want to finish today's habit”
- “I wanted a habit tracker that celebrates consistency”
- “When checking a box isn't motivating enough”
- “A habit tracker for people emotionally attached to their streaks”
- “Keep the streak. Skip the subscription.”
- “This is what 30 days of small wins looks like”

Use time and money in this order:

1. Improve the store listing.
2. Personally recruit and learn from testers.
3. Produce reusable short demo clips.
4. Add shareable achievement and streak cards.
5. Participate helpfully in relevant communities.
6. Cross-promote through Metamodern Monkey.
7. Launch on Product Hunt and Indie Hackers.
8. Pitch small Android, productivity, and self-improvement creators.
9. Contact small newsletters, blogs, podcasts, and app-review channels.
10. Test paid promotion only after retention is promising.

For creator outreach:

- [ ] Build a list of 30 small relevant creators, ideally with 1,000–25,000 followers.
- [ ] Reference a specific video, article, or audience interest in each message.
- [ ] Explain why streak motivation or light gamification suits their audience.
- [ ] Offer early access or a permanent Pro code.
- [ ] Invite an honest review rather than requiring a positive one.
- [ ] Follow up once, then stop.

Do not buy reviews or incentivized installs. Avoid broad advertising initially; paid
traffic will not compensate for weak onboarding or retention.

If paid promotion is tested later:

- [ ] Set a hard total budget of $50–$100.
- [ ] Test one channel and one message at a time.
- [ ] Advertise a concrete hook such as “turn small wins into motivating streaks.”
- [ ] Use a dedicated landing-page URL or Play Store experiment.
- [ ] Compare retained users, not just click or install costs.
- [ ] Stop the experiment if users do not remain active after one and four weeks.

## Measurement

The marketing measurement approach should respect the product's privacy promise. Start
with aggregate Play Console reports, landing-page counts, voluntary surveys, and manual
campaign tracking instead of installing an invasive behavioral analytics SDK.

Track:

- Store-listing visitors and install conversion
- Installs and uninstalls
- One-week and four-week retention
- Ratings and recurring review themes
- Testers who create at least one real habit
- Testers who remain active through the full test
- Users who describe streaks or achievements as motivating
- Shared-card link visits after sharing exists
- Users reaching the six-active-habit limit
- Eventual one-time Pro conversion

Review these once per week. Decide on one experiment for the following week, such as a
new store screenshot, video hook, community, or landing-page headline. Avoid changing
several variables simultaneously when the goal is to learn what worked.
